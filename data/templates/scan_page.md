The page is from The Century Dictionary and Cyclopedia (New York, 1897), set in
three columns of small type.

An entry begins with its title-word in bold at the margin, often followed by a
superior figure that tells it from another word spelled the same (bating¹,
bating²): write that figure as <sup>1</sup>. After the title-word comes its
pronunciation in parentheses, then the part of speech in italic, then the
etymology in square brackets, then the numbered senses.

The numbers of the senses are printed in bold, every one, the first after the
etymology and each after a dash or at a line's start: write them bold, a
dagger with its number ('''1.''' To detach; —'''2.''' The place; '''3†.''' To
reject). So is the synonyms' head with its number: ='''Syn. 1.''' A number
in a cross-reference (see ''abbas'', 1) or a citation (Rev. ix. 11) is not
bold.

Pronunciations are respellings in the book's own notation: transcribe every
mark exactly, choosing each character from the book's key below, with the
accent marks ′ and ″ as printed. Greek is printed in Greek type: write it in
Greek characters with its accents and breathings, never in italic. The signs
< > + = √ * † ‖ are printed as they are and transcribed as they are.

A dagger (†) right after a title-word marks it obsolete: '''aban†''',
'''abbas†'''. In this type it looks much like a final t — a title-word that
seems to end in a small raised t is ending in †. Write the dagger, never t.

Quotations follow a sense in smaller type, each on its own lines, with its
source set flush right beneath it.

Quotation marks and apostrophes are printed curly: write them curly, “ ” ‘ ’
(don’t, “abduct”), never the straight " or '.

Each letter of the alphabet opens with a large ornamental capital, several lines
tall, set into the first lines of its first entry (the A beside "1. The first
letter in the English alphabet"). It is that letter, not an illustration:
transcribe it as the letter, in bold, where the entry begins ('''A'''), never
as a figure.
