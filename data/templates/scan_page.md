The page is from The Century Dictionary and Cyclopedia (New York, 1897), set in
three columns of small type.

An entry begins with its title-word in bold at the margin, often followed by a
superior figure that tells it from another word spelled the same (bating¹,
bating²): write that figure as <sup>1</sup>. After the title-word comes its
pronunciation in parentheses, then the part of speech in italic, then the
etymology in square brackets, then the numbered senses.

Pronunciations are respellings in the book's own notation: transcribe every
mark exactly, choosing each character from the book's key below, with the
accent marks ′ and ″ as printed. Greek is printed in Greek type: write it in
Greek characters with its accents and breathings, never in italic. The signs
< > + = √ * † ‖ are printed as they are and transcribed as they are.

Quotations follow a sense in smaller type, each on its own lines, with its
source set flush right beneath it.
